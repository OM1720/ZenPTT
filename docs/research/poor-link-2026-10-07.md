Purpose: record measured results, the selected transport behavior, and remaining uncertainty.

Audience: maintainers, protocol developers, and testers.

Authority: dated experimental evidence only; [`../protocol.md`](../protocol.md) defines the current wire and client behavior.

## Method and completion rule

Two independent Linux headless bots used isolated channels through the test host. Network shaping, ingress policing, delay, loss, and blackouts applied inside test containers. The source was a pinned LibriSpeech sample, prepared as 16 kHz mono PCM; its SHA-256 and CC BY 4.0 attribution are in `scripts/poor-link/audio-source.json`. Each direction sent three consecutive 20-second bursts with a two-second pause after each send, without waiting for the receiver. Each burst planned 1,000 Opus frames of 20 ms. The fixed seeds were `1009`, `2017`, and `3037`.

The R/A/B/AB comparison used 17 profiles, three repeats, and both directions: 102 cases per variant, 408 in total. A case is **complete** only when all three bursts have the planned, produced, and uniquely received frames with no unresolved measurement or protocol failure. A `complete` terminal label or cumulative ACK alone does not prove delivery of the planned speech. Sender shaping and listener policing measure different bottlenecks; listener ingress shaping was evaluated separately. Interface traffic includes TCP overhead and was sampled over each bot interval. Server timestamps were correlated with clock-offset estimates, not treated as perfectly synchronized client timestamps. No quality threshold was assumed in advance.

## Four-way protocol comparison

| Variant | Ordinary ACK | Fresh uplink frames per envelope | Complete cases | Baseline median sender traffic |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| R | Original | One | 58/102 | 429.2 KiB |
| A | Coalesced, up to 100 ms | One | 57/102 | 424.6 KiB |
| B | Original | Up to three, at most 40 ms waiting | **81/102** | **239.2 KiB** |
| AB | Coalesced, up to 100 ms | Up to three, at most 40 ms waiting | 78/102 | 236.9 KiB |

Grouping reduced sampled baseline sender traffic by 44.3% from R to B. At 32 kbit/s sender shaping, B and AB completed 6/6 cases each; R and A completed 0/6. At 24 kbit/s listener policing, B completed 5/6 and AB 3/6. This boundary was noisy; the comparison does not establish that ACK coalescing caused AB's lower result. ACK coalescing reduced ACK count, but showed no delivery advantage. R, A, B, and AB used the same diagnostic changes.

## Lease and floor competition

A separate three-bot trial used **AB** for every lease candidate. The third bot requested the floor when the second burst's sender blackout began and retried about once per second. Each cell shows complete second bursts out of six cases.

| Renewal lease | 1.25 s blackout | 1.5 s | 2 s | 3 s | Median contender wait after sender disappearance |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 s | 4/6 | 0/6 | 0/6 | 0/6 | 2.10 s |
| 3 s | 6/6 | 0/6 | 0/6 | 0/6 | 3.16 s |
| 4 s | 6/6 | 6/6 | 6/6 | 0/6 | 4.27 s |

All 72 blackout cases had network counters and corresponding server evidence; no simultaneous floor owners were observed. Separate disappearance, voluntary completion, and cancellation checks found prompt release for the latter two. A four-second lease preserved the second burst across a two-second blackout in this setup, while a disappeared sender held the floor about 2.17 seconds longer than with a two-second lease. These exact lease results were measured on AB, not independently on B.

The user selected **B with the original ACK behavior and two-second renewal lease**. This exact combination completed 81/102 cases in the main matrix. It favors quicker floor release and accepts interrupted speech on longer blackouts. Initial grant remains five seconds, recovery horizon five seconds, and absolute burst limit 60 seconds. Android, browser, and headless now group fresh frames; recovery sends ready retained ranges without waiting for a fresh group. The v4 envelope and server ACK contract did not change.

## Browser playback under jitter

The selected package was also exercised through two separate Linux proxies with real browser AudioWorklet and Opus paths. These short browser bursts changed the TCP path and are **not** part of the direct bot matrix. The corrected study has 18 valid runs, or 36 measured directions: baseline and unstable links, three seeds, both directions, and test-only extra prebuffer of 0/50/100 ms. Every valid direction had matching sent, received, and played frame identities.

| Link | Extra test prebuffer | Directions | Internal PCM silence | AudioWorklet queue blocks | Median first receipt to first play |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 0 ms | 6 | 0 ms | 0 | 117.8 ms |
| Baseline | 50 ms | 6 | 0 ms | 0 | 171.9 ms |
| Baseline | 100 ms | 6 | 0 ms | 0 | 222.5 ms |
| Unstable | 0 ms | 6 | 1,254 ms | 15 | 118.2 ms |
| Unstable | 50 ms | 6 | 685 ms | 15 | 178.3 ms |
| Unstable | 100 ms | 6 | 390 ms | 7 | 222.2 ms |

Relative to the unstable zero-prebuffer group, 50 ms reduced total measured internal silence by 45% and 100 ms by 69%. Baseline median startup delay increased by 54.1 and 104.7 ms respectively. Neither candidate removed all queue blocks. A second six-run matrix without the ScriptProcessorNode PCM observer still recorded 20 blocks totaling 1,331.8 ms on unstable links, compared with one 10.1 ms block in baseline. The worklet stall is therefore observable without that heavy observer. The PCM duration oracle also flagged some baseline runs without queue blocks; its error alone is not proof of packet loss or audible degradation.

The prebuffer was a test-only delay before the unchanged worklet startup delay, not a production setting. Two first attempts were incomplete and excluded from the valid matrix: one browser capture failed to reach its frame target and reported an audio-stop error; another timed out waiting for Disconnect with unequal frame counts. An identical retry of each completed. Their causes remain unresolved. An earlier exploratory matrix used a faulty end condition and was excluded after the browser test began waiting for the explicit worklet burst-end marker.

The full local gate, complete four-file package gate, and 85/85 API 35 connected Android tests passed. A bot control on the selected package delivered 6,000/6,000 planned frames in both directions. The mixed APK/browser check recorded 138 Android frames received and played by the browser, and 224 browser Opus frames corresponding to 215,040 Android PCM samples. The test host health check passed after acceptance. Later test and documentation changes did not alter the production files in the 198-file saved source snapshot; they still match the validated package. Raw per-case measurements and logs remain in ignored `acceptance/artifacts/poor-link/` paths, including the comparison JSON files and browser `analysis.json`.

The mixed APK/browser result came from a local Windows helper tied to an API 35 emulator with a 1080 × 2400 display and fixed touch coordinates. That helper remains outside the public test suite. It compared aggregate frame counts and Android playback duration (960 PCM samples per 20 ms Opus frame at 48 kHz), without verifying individual frame identities. Its result is limited to that setup and does not establish portable mixed-client regression coverage.

Automated tests do not establish audible speech quality, physical BM008/SCO routing, real microphone behavior, or mouth-to-ear latency. The user excluded physical testing from this acceptance; it remains unverified. Rare browser capture-stop/disconnect failures and jitter-induced playback stalls warrant further investigation before claiming smooth audio on unstable links. No permanent browser buffer increase or ACK change was selected.
